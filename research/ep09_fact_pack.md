**Avoid:**
- Saying 鎌首 means the head. Sources use 鎌 / 鎌頭 for the head and 首 / 鎌首 for the neck. The 鎌首 image is a snake's raised neck and head.
- "It looks like a sickle blade" or "named after a sickle's shape". Sources link the name to 鎌首 (a snake's raised head), not to the blade.
- Implying the Japanese name means "goose". "Gooseneck" is an English rendering.
- Calling it a half-lap. JAANUS's "half-lap" wording follows its lap-joint glossary; the seat is not a half-lap splice.
- Saying the head runs the full depth. It is about the upper half, with the seat below.
- Saying it slides in sideways, or that the female goes on top.
- Saying the 目違い makes it stronger in tension. It resists twisting and out-of-plane shear.
- Saying it is moment-resisting or "rigid". It is treated as a pin.
- "Kama is X times stronger than ari" as a rule. Give numbers only with specimen context (R9-39/40/43).
- "A longer head is proportionally stronger." Stress concentrates near the neck; depth matters more.
- Any "standard" head, neck or seat dimension. The 30 / 7.5 / 15 mm figures are test-specimen values; the R9-45 range is an engineering applicability range.
- The 1/10 すべり勾配 unless attributed to one test drawing.
- Saying MLIT specifies kama for framing. MLIT leaves shape to 特記 and mentions 鎌継手 only for log walls.
- Saying the Flat 35 spec allows kama for 大引き, or that it allows ari for 胴差し.
- Saying Flat 35 requires hardware on the sill or 胴差し kama splice. Hardware is named only for 小屋ばり, 2階床ばり and 母屋.
- Putting the anchor bolt in the female (lower) piece.
- "Used since the 7th century" stated as fact (JAANUS only).
- "kamazugi" / かまづぎ.
- 大鎌継 / 真鎌継 and the "chigiritsugi" aside from JAANUS.
- Citing the MHLW manual for this joint.
