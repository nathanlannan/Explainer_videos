# Episode 6 — Marking Out and Cutting the Koshikake Ari-tsugi · final script

Runtime 4:42.3 · 512 words · timestamps are narration start–end in the final cut.


## 1. Cold open

**0:00.8–0:11.9** (N01) Last time, the shape. This time, how a carpenter marks it out and cuts it, so the upper piece drops in and tightens as it seats.

**0:12.3–0:19.6** (N02) We'll follow one training course's sequence, step by step. Others may order things differently.


## 2. Before marking

**0:25.1–0:39.9** (N03) First, decide which piece is which. The male is the upper piece, and it's the one that takes the anchor bolt, beside the joint. Mark each piece clearly before you lay anything out.

**0:40.6–0:52.7** (N04) Then the center lines, as in Episode 1. The dovetail is laid out from them, evenly on either side, so they need to be right before anything else.


## 3. Marking the female

**0:54.9–1:08.9** (N05) On the female piece, start on the top face with the end line. Then the seat lines. Then the dovetail lines, laid out evenly on either side of the center line.

**1:09.3–1:24.1** (N06) The sides of the dovetail are angled using the width of the sashigane's blade, running from the seat line toward the dovetail line. Finally, the seat lines are carried down both side faces.


## 4. Marking the male

**1:28.3–1:35.0** (N07) On the male piece: the end line, the tip of the dovetail, and the cut lines.


## 5. Cutting the female

**1:38.2–1:53.9** (N08) Now cut the female. Rip in from the end along the seat line, splitting it. Then crosscut down to it, also splitting the line, and the waste above the seat comes away.

**1:54.3–2:04.1** (N09) Rip the sides of the dovetail socket, splitting the line, without sawing past the end of the dovetail or into the seat below.

**2:04.5–2:17.7** (N10) Chop out the socket, keeping the chisel's flat back toward the outside of the socket. Then work down to the level of the seat.

**2:18.1–2:31.6** (N11) One detail matters: at the tip, dig the socket about three millimeters deeper than the line. Then the tip can't touch first and leave a gap somewhere else in the joint.


## 6. Cutting the male

**2:35.3–2:56.1** (N12) Now the male. First cut the end square at the end line, then carry the center line and the dovetail lines onto the fresh end grain. Rip in to the shoulder, then crosscut away the lower half, leaving the cut-back underside that will sit on the seat.

**2:56.5–3:13.6** (N13) Rip the cheeks of the dovetail with a slight taper: leave the line at the top, and take it at the bottom. The dovetail is a hair narrower below, so it enters easily and tightens as it goes down.

**3:14.0–3:24.7** (N14) Crosscut the shoulders, splitting the line. Then chisel away what the saw couldn't reach in the corners, and chamfer the edges with the chisel.


## 7. Test fit

**3:28.4–3:46.6** (N15) Test the fit by dropping the male in from above. If it's a little too tight, a general carpentry manual suggests kigoroshi. The gennō's slightly domed face carries the same name: the kigoroshi face.

**3:47.0–3:54.9** (N16) And remember why the tip was dug deep: the joint should bear everywhere else, never on its tip.


## 8. Next time

**3:58.6–4:04.7** (N17) Next time, a new joint: the mortise and tenon, hozo and hozo-ana.


## 9. Recap and a note

**4:05.7–4:17.4** (N18) Center lines first. Mark the female, then the male. Cut to the lines you chose, taper the cheeks, and let it drop in and tighten.

**4:27.3–4:37.9** (N19) One note: sharp-tool technique, and how to use these tools safely, is best learned from a teacher, at a bench, in person.
