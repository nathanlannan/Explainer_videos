## 1. Cold open

**0:00.8–0:16.5** (N01) The upper piece is lowered onto the lower, and the pegs go in. This time we mark one piece out and cut it, step by step, following one training course.

**0:16.9–0:26.0** (N02) The course marks and cuts the lower piece first. The upper piece is the same shape, but its slope runs the other way.


## 2. Marking out

**0:31.3–0:40.3** (N03) Marking starts on the top face: the end line, then the line for the housing that will take the other piece's tongue.

**0:40.7–0:49.7** (N04) Next, the shoulder lines and the line along the beam, then the other shoulder, with marks to locate the slope.

**0:50.1–1:03.4** (N05) Using those marks, the slope line goes on the top face and on one side face, with that face's shoulder lines. The underside is then marked the same way as the top.

**1:03.8–1:20.8** (N06) On the other side face goes the splice's center line, and the peg center lines are set out evenly either side of it. For some of the peg lines, the course uses the sashigane's blade width as a quick gauge.


## 3. Cutting: first stages

**1:25.0–1:33.3** (N07) Cutting the lower piece. Cut the end at the end line, and mark the end grain.

**1:33.7–1:47.3** (N08) Most of these cuts leave half the line: the edge of the cut falls on the middle of the ink line, so half of it stays on the piece. Episode ten did the same.

**1:47.7–1:56.5** (N09) Before sawing the joint, the course chops the peg holes from the side face, about a quarter of the width deep.

**1:56.9–2:12.1** (N10) Then it rips along the beam from the end, leaving half the line, to just past the slope, and crosscuts at the slope. The first block of waste comes away.

**2:12.5–2:29.0** (N11) It crosscuts the shoulder, leaving half the line, down to just short of the line along the beam. Then it drives a chisel into the end grain at the slope cut, and splits that waste off, back to the shoulder.


## 4. The slope, housing and tongue

**2:29.7–2:52.2** (N12) Mark the slope again, on the newly split face. Saw it, leaving half the line, to just short of the line along the beam. Then add kerfs about a chisel's width apart, back to the shoulder, careful not to saw too deep. Chop them out, and pare, leaving half the line.

**2:52.6–3:07.8** (N13) Next, the housing at the shoulder: kerfs, then the chisel. Then the tongue that goes into the other piece's housing: a rip and a crosscut, leaving half the line.

**3:08.2–3:14.1** (N14) Last, it marks the peg holes on the newly cut faces and chops them.

**3:14.5–3:19.9** (N15) The upper piece is the same shape, cut with its slope running the other way.


## 5. Assembly

**3:24.1–3:32.3** (N16) Then the upper piece is lowered onto the lower, and the two pegs go in from opposite sides.

**3:32.7–3:48.7** (N17) One test database warns against a fit so tight it has to be driven in. It suggests a fit where light taps bring the two pieces level, with the pegs there to stop them slipping.


## 6. The series

**3:51.4–4:03.1** (N18) That's the end of this series: from the story stick and the marking kit, through the saws and chisels, to three splices, a crossing joint and a tenon.


## 7. Recap

**4:06.9–4:21.0** (N19) Mark the lower piece first, chop the peg holes early, take the waste off in stages, cut the slope, and make the upper the same, with its slope reversed.
