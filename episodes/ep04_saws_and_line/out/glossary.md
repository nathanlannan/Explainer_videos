# Glossary: Episode 4 · Part 1, Saws and the Line

Kanji appear only for terms verified in independent sources (see `research/ep04_fact_pack.md`, rows R5-xx). The three line terms rest on a single (authoritative) source, so they are given in romaji only.

| Romaji | Kanji | Kana | English gloss | One-line description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ryōba | 両刃 (両刃鋸) | りょうば | double-edged saw | One rip edge and one crosscut edge. A Meiji-era design (late 1800s). (R5-01, R5-03) |
| tatebiki | 縦挽き | たてびき | rip | Cutting along the grain; the teeth work like a row of tiny chisels. (R5-01, R5-02) |
| yokobiki | 横挽き | よこびき | crosscut | Cutting across the grain; the teeth are pointed like small knives and sever the fibers. (R5-01, R5-02) |
| dōzuki | 胴付鋸 | どうづき | backed joinery saw | A very thin blade stiffened by a metal spine (背金). Classed as a crosscut saw. (R5-04, R5-06) |
| dōzuki | 胴付 | どうづき | tenon shoulder | The face around the base of a tenon. (R5-05) |
| kataba | 片刃鋸 | かたば | single-edged saw | One edge, no spine. (R5-07) |
| — | — | — | pull stroke | Japanese saws cut on the pull, which puts the blade in tension so it can be made thinner. A pushed blade generally needs to be stiffer. (R5-08–R5-10) |
| — | — | — | kerf | The width of a saw cut: the blade plus the set. (R5-11) |
| asari | — | あさり | set | Teeth bent out alternately, left and right, so the kerf is wider than the blade. (R5-11) |
| sumi o harau / nokosu / matagu | — | すみをはらう / のこす / またぐ | take / leave / split the line | Where the kerf goes relative to the line: it removes the line from the piece; it is all in the waste; or it is centered on the line. The skills-test manual's three options, chosen by how tight each fit should be. (R5-12–R5-14) |
