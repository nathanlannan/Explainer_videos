# Episode 10 — Cutting the Gooseneck · final script

Runtime 5:00.7 · 563 words · timestamps are narration start–end in the final cut.


## 1. Cold open

**0:00.8–0:15.2** (N01) The male drops in from above, and the gooseneck seats. This time we mark it out and cut it, step by step, following one training course.

**0:15.6–0:27.0** (N02) That course's exercise adds a small stub tenon at the seat, to stop the pieces twisting. We'll leave it out and follow the steps for the joint itself.


## 2. Before marking

**0:32.3–0:42.1** (N03) First, mark which piece is which. The male is the upper piece; on a sill, the anchor bolt goes in it, beside the splice.

**0:42.5–0:51.6** (N04) Both pieces already carry center lines, as in episode one. The neck and head are laid out from them.


## 3. Marking the female

**0:53.8–1:04.9** (N05) On the female, the course marks the end line first. Then the seat lines, the lines for the neck and the head's tip, and the cut line.

**1:05.3–1:17.0** (N06) The neck lines are split evenly from the center line. The course notes that a sashigane's blade is about fifteen millimeters wide, which makes these lines quick to mark.

**1:17.4–1:29.8** (N07) The head's slanted lines also use the blade's width, running from the tip toward the neck. Then the seat lines are carried down both side faces.


## 4. Marking the male

**1:34.0–1:48.9** (N08) The male is marked the same way: the end line, the neck and tip lines and the cut line, the neck lines from the center, the slanted head lines, and the seat lines down both sides.


## 5. Cutting the female

**1:52.1–2:11.2** (N09) Cutting the female. Cut the end at the end line and mark the end grain. Rip in, leaving half the line, angling the saw so it doesn't damage the seat. Then crosscut, leaving half the line, to form the seat.

**2:11.6–2:25.3** (N10) Chop the head's tip straight down, taking the line. Then chop along both sides, using the saw kerfs as a guide, and clear out the socket from the side.

**2:25.7–2:37.0** (N11) The course digs a little past the line at the tip, about three millimeters, and checks that the floor is flat and level with the seat.

**2:37.4–2:55.0** (N12) Along the head's sides, it chops leaving half the line. Where the head meets the neck, it stops about three millimeters short of the line, on the tip side, then pares that strip into a slight slope on the bearing faces.


## 6. Cutting the male

**2:59.2–3:15.0** (N13) Cutting the male. Cut the end at the end line and mark the end grain. Rip in along the face that will rest on the seat, then crosscut the shoulder, leaving half the line on both.

**3:15.4–3:31.3** (N14) Rip the head's sides down to the neck, leaving the line at the top and taking it at the bottom. The head is a hair narrower below, so it enters easily and gets snug as it goes down.

**3:31.7–3:45.6** (N15) Crosscut the shoulder, stopping short of the neck line, and the waste beside the head splits off with the chisel. Saw the bearing slope, leaving half the line.

**3:46.0–4:03.2** (N16) Add kerfs about a chisel's width apart beside the neck, and chop the neck, leaving half the line, with the same taper. Finally, pare the bearing slope, and chamfer the bottom edges of the head and neck.


## 7. Assembly

**4:07.4–4:11.6** (N17) Then the male drops in from above, onto the seat.
