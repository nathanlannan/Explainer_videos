| S06 Cutting the female | 1:51.8 | 67.1 s | threeQ | End cut; rip angled clear of the seat; crosscut to form the seat; chop the tip, the sides, clear the socket; tip dug ~3 mm past the line; inset: the neck-end strip left, then pared to a slope (schematic). Study hold 3.5 s. | Cutting the female. Cut the end at the end line and mark the end grain. Rip in, leaving half the line, angling the saw so it doesn't damage the seat. Then crosscut, leaving half the line, to form the seat. Chop the head's tip straight down, taking the line. Then chop along both sides, using the saw kerfs as a guide, and clear out the socket from the side. The course digs a little past the line at the tip, about three millimeters, and checks that the floor is flat and level with the seat. Along the head's sides, it chops leaving half the line. Where the head meets the neck, it stops about three millimeters short of the line, on the tip side, then pares that strip into a slight slope on the bearing faces. |
| S07 Cutting the male | 2:58.9 | 68.2 s | threeQ | End cut; rip along the seat face, crosscut the shoulder; head sides ripped with the entry taper (inset, exaggerated); waste split off; bearing slope sawn; kerfs beside the neck, neck chopped; slope pared; underside edges chamfered. Study hold 3.5 s. | Cutting the male. Cut the end at the end line and mark the end grain. Rip in along the face that will rest on the seat, then crosscut the shoulder, leaving half the line on both. Rip the head's sides down to the neck, leaving the line at the top and taking it at the bottom. The head is a hair narrower below, so it enters easily and gets snug as it goes down. Crosscut the shoulder, stopping short of the neck line, and the waste beside the head splits off with the chisel. Saw the bearing slope, leaving half the line. Add kerfs about a chisel's width apart beside the neck, and chop the neck, leaving half the line, with the same taper. Finally, pare the bearing slope, and chamfer the bottom edges of the head and neck. |
