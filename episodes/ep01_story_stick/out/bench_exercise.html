Bench exercise: one stick, four legs

Japanese Joinery by Hand · Episode 1 · Make a story stick (bakabō 馬鹿棒) for a small project and transfer it to four parts. About 45 minutes. No joinery is cut; this is layout only.

You need

  • 4 leg blanks for a small stool or side table, about 45 × 45 mm (1¾ in) and a little over the finished length
  • 1 straight, plain batten, longer than the legs (scrap is fine: 30–45 mm wide, 10–15 mm thick)
  • A square, a sharp pencil or marking knife, and a tape or rule used once only, on the stick

1 · Make the stick

  1. Square one end of the batten. That end is the stick's reference end.
  2. From that end, measure each position once and square a line across the stick.
  3. Beside every line, write a short note saying what it is for. Mark the side of each line the part goes on.
  4. Put the tape away. You will not need it again.

2 · Transfer to four legs

  1. Pick one end of each leg as its bottom (reference) end and mark it.
  2. Lay the stick against leg 1 with the reference ends exactly flush. Clamp it, or hold it firmly.
  3. At each line, square across from the stick onto the leg. Copy each note too.
  4. Repeat on legs 2, 3 and 4. Use the same stick, the same reference end, and the same face of the stick against the leg.

3 · Check your work: the payoff

  • Stand the four legs side by side with their bottom ends against a straightedge or fence. Lay a long square across all four at each mark: the lines should run straight across all four legs.
  • For comparison, lay out a fifth blank with the tape, measuring each mark from the last one. Put it beside the others and look at the top marks.
  • Remember the point: the stick doesn't make the legs accurate, it makes them identical. If the stick is 1 mm off your plan, all four legs are 1 mm off together, and the joints still agree.

Common slips

  • Ends not flush. Any offset at the reference end moves every mark on that leg. Butt both ends against a stop block.
  • The stick turned end for end or flipped. Write "BOTTOM" big on the stick's reference end.
  • A slipping stick. Clamp it. A shifted stick makes one leg disagree with the rest.

Optional, toward Episode 2: also strike a center line down one face of each leg and the stick. In Japanese layout, the center line is the main reference (shinzumi 芯墨). Marking and cutting tools are taught in later episodes.
