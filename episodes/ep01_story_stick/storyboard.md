| S04 Two workflows | 0:23.6 | 32.4 s | 2d | Split screen. Left: tape hooks, reads, marks each piece in turn. Right: one stick, marks copied to all pieces. Study hold 0.9 s. | Most of us lay out with a tape. Hook the end, read the number, make a mark. Then the next piece, and the next. Every piece gets its own measurement, and every measurement is a fresh chance to be slightly off. Traditional Japanese carpentry leans the other way. Each position is decided once, on a single reference, and copied onto every piece. After that, nothing is measured again. |
| S05 Sumitsuke | 0:56.0 | 15.1 s | front | Close on a post face: an ink line drawn across. Label: sumitsuke 墨付け / layout marking. Study hold 1.3 s. | This marking out of the timber is called sumitsuke: drawing every line in ink before a saw touches the wood. It is where the fit of a joint is decided. |
| S06 Timber varies | 1:11.1 | 17.2 s | 2d | End-grain sections: 105 vs 99 mm; out of square (exaggerated); shrinkage as it dries. | But copying needs something to copy from. Real timber is never quite the size on the drawing. One post comes out a few millimeters thinner than the next. It may not be square, and it moves as it dries. |
| S07 Edge vs center line | 1:28.3 | 45.3 s | 2d | Plan view, four posts of differing size. Top: laid out from the front edge, joint marks miss the shared line (✕). Bottom: from the center line, marks on the line (✓). Study hold 4.0 s. | Lay out from a face, and that face lines up perfectly, but every difference in size lands on the other side. A post a few millimeters thin ends up with its middle off the line its neighbors share. On a Japanese plan, the lines that place the posts and walls usually run through the middle of the members. So the carpenter lays out from the middle too: each member gets a center line, the shinzumi, struck in ink. A thinner post still has its center on the line. The difference splits, half to each side. |

| S12 Tape drift vs stick | 3:35.8 | 31.1 s | 2d | Four posts, marks from chained tape readings, drift drawn x20 (labelled). Then four story-stick posts: zero spread. Study hold 4.5 s. | Now the worst case with a tape. Each position is measured from the last, and each reading is off by up to a millimeter either way, drawn here twenty times larger. The errors add up along the post, and every post adds them up differently. And here are the four story stick posts, on the same scale. No difference between them at all. |
| S13 Identical, not accurate | 4:06.9 | 23.0 s | 2d | If the stick is 1 mm off, all four posts shift together. Key line in type. And identical is what joinery needs: a joint only cares that its two pieces agree. |
