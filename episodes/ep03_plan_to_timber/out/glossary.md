# Glossary: Episode 3, From Plan to Timber

Kanji appear only for terms verified in independent sources (see `research/research_table.md`, rows R4-xx).

| Romaji | Kanji | Kana | English gloss | One-line description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| itazu | 板図 (also 図板 zuita, 手板 teita) | いたず | board plan | The carpenter's own plan of the building, drawn on a thin board (often plywood today) from the architect's drawings. It works like a floor plan and the framing plans combined. (R4-01, R4-02) |
| — | — | — | grid | Lines usually every 3 shaku (about 910 mm), with posts at the crossings. Each grid line runs through the centres of the posts on it. (R4-04, R4-21) |
| banzuke | 番付 | ばんづけ | grid addresses | Marks on posts, beams and other members, and the system behind them. Classic form: iroha (い ろ は に) one way, numbers (一 二 三) the other. A common layout runs iroha across and numbers down, but the axes and starting corner vary by region and carpenter. Other systems exist. (R4-07–R4-12) |
| i-no-ichi | い一 | いのいち | an address | The post at grid lines い and 一. (R4-11) |
| — | — | — | where the address goes | Written by hand while marking out, where it will be hidden once built: on a tenon, or on the part of a post below the floor. In one common practice, a member meeting post い一 is marked い一 at that joint. (R4-14–R4-16) |
| kizami | 刻み | きざみ | cutting | Cutting the members after marking out. (R4-18) |
| sumitsuke | 墨付け | すみつけ | marking out | From Episode 1: marking lines and marks in ink. |
| shaku | 尺 | しゃく | old unit | About 303 mm; 3 shaku ≈ 910 mm. |
