| S09 Why root two | 2:07.4 | 39.0 s | 2d | A 100 mm square, its diagonal 141.4 mm; s² + s² = d²; the back scale laid along the diagonal reads 100. Study hold 4.0 s. | Why would anyone want a stretched ruler? Because of one shape that is everywhere in timber: the square. A square's diagonal is always its side times the square root of two. So measure a diagonal with the back scale, and the number you read is the side. A square one hundred millimeters on a side has a diagonal of one hundred forty-one point four. On the back scale, that same length reads one hundred. |
| S10 Log to largest beam | 2:46.4 | 49.0 s | front | Log end, 300 mm: inscribed square, corners on the circle; diagonal = diameter; back scale across reads 212; square inked. Study hold 4.5 s. | Back to the log. Measure at the small end, because a log tapers and the small end limits the beam. The biggest square you can cut there has its corners on the circle, so its diagonal is the log's diameter. Lay the back scale across the small end, straight through the center, and read it at the far edge. Two hundred twelve. That's the side of the largest square beam this log can give: two hundred twelve millimeters. No calculator, no pencil arithmetic, no chance to slip a digit. The arithmetic is built into the tool. |
