Bench exercise: make a back scale and read a log

Japanese Joinery by Hand · Episode 2 · Part 1. You don't need a sashigane: you'll make a paper ura-me 裏目 (√2 scale) and check, with your own hands, that it reads the largest square in a circle. About 30 minutes.

Print at 100% / "actual size" (not "fit to page"). Then check the 100 mm calibration bar with a ruler. If it isn't 100 mm, the scales below are wrong; reprint before using them.

1 · Prove the √2 on paper

  1. Draw a square 100 mm on a side, with a good square and a sharp pencil.
  2. Measure its diagonal with an ordinary ruler. You should get about 141 mm (100 × 1.414).
  3. Now lay the back scale strip along the same diagonal. It reads 100: the side.

2 · Read a "log"

  1. Find something round with a flat end: a log or branch end, a dowel, a jar lid, a pipe.
  2. Lay the back scale across the end, straight through the center, zero on one edge. Read it at the far edge. That number is the side of the largest square you could cut from it.
  3. For a real log, measure at the small end, since a log tapers.

3 · Check it

  1. Trace the round end on paper. Draw two diameters at right angles through the center (fold the paper, or use a square).
  2. Join the four points where they meet the circle. That's the largest inscribed square. Measure its side with an ordinary ruler: it should match your back-scale reading, within about 1 mm.
  3. Try a square 1 mm bigger: its corners poke outside the circle.

4 · Optional: the round scale

  • Lay the marume strip across the same diameter and read it. Then wrap a string around the object and measure the string with an ordinary ruler. The two numbers should agree: the round scale reads the circumference.

What you just did

A square's diagonal is its side × √2. A scale stretched by √2 turns any diagonal straight into the side, with no arithmetic. Across a circle, the diameter is the inscribed square's diagonal. That's all the sashigane's back scale is doing.

The strips are for learning, not precision work: paper stretches and printers vary. On a real sashigane, scale placement varies by model; one common layout has the ura-me on the back of the long arm's outer edge and the marume on the back of the short arm.
