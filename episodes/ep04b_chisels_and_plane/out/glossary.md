# Glossary: Episode 4 · Part 2, Chisels, Hammer and Plane

Kanji appear only for verified terms (see `research/ep04_fact_pack.md`, rows R5-xx). katsura is given in kana only (its kanji is unconfirmed).

| Romaji | Kanji | Kana | English gloss | One-line description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nomi | 鑿 | のみ | chisel | Two families: tataki-nomi 叩き鑿 (struck with a hammer) and tsuki-nomi 突き鑿 (pushed by hand). (R5-15) |
| katsura | — | かつら | hoop | The iron hoop fitted around the end of a struck chisel's handle. (R5-18) |
| oire-nomi | 追入鑿 | おいれのみ | bench chisel | The general struck bench chisel; also read oiire, or written ōire 大入. (R5-16) |
| mukōmachi-nomi | 向待鑿 | むこうまちのみ | mortise chisel | A thick blade, for deep mortises. (R5-17) |
| hagane / jigane | 鋼 / 地金 | はがね / じがね | steel / iron | A laminated blade: a thin layer of hard steel forge-welded to a thicker body of softer iron. The iron supports the brittle steel and absorbs shock. (R5-19, R5-28) |
| — | 片刃 | かたば | single bevel | Woodworking chisels are bevelled on one side only. (R5-27) |
| urasuki | 裏スキ | うらすき | back hollow | A shallow hollow in the back that makes it easier to flatten. The bevel is flat, not hollow-ground. (R5-20) |
| gennō | 玄能 | げんのう | hammer | One flat face and one slightly domed face. (R5-21) |
| kigoroshi-men | 木殺し面 | きごろしめん | domed face | Gives the last blows when driving a nail, so the surface isn't marked. Literally "wood-killing face". (R5-21) |
| kanna | 鉋 | かんな | plane | Pulled toward you. Western and Chinese planes are pushed. (R5-22) |
| dai | 台 | だい | block | The wooden block the blade sits in, usually oak. (R5-23) |
| uragane | 裏金 | うらがね | chip breaker | The second iron behind the blade; helps stop tear-out. (R5-24) |
| nimai-ganna | 二枚鉋 | にまいがんな | two-blade plane | A Meiji-era development. (R5-24) |
| — | — | — | setting the blade | In the national skills-test manual: tap the blade's head to set it deeper; tap the corners of the block's head end to draw it back. (R5-25) |
